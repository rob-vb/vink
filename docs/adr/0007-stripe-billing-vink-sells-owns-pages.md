# Stripe owns the billing, Vink is the seller and owns the Pages

Status: superseded in part by [ADR 0010](0010-any-input-routed-to-a-form.md) (2026-10-08): the billing unit is now the Item (1 PDF page, 1 email or 1 photo), not the Page. The Stripe design, and Vink owning the allowance, still hold; read Pages below as Items.

Supersedes [ADR 0006](0006-polar-owns-billing-vink-owns-pages.md): Vink bills through its own Stripe account instead of through Polar. The split between Subscription (Stripe) and Pages (Vink) still holds.

Paid Plans and Top-ups go through Stripe: Checkout to start a Plan or buy Top-ups, the Customer Portal to change Plan, cancel, update the payment method, address and VAT number, and see invoices. Custom Plans stay by hand (`pages:setPlan`), paid by invoice.

**Vink is the seller**, not a merchant of record: no Polar, no Stripe Managed Payments. We tried both (branches `stripe-billing` and `polar-billing`); both chose the payment methods themselves, and neither offered iDEAL or SEPA Direct Debit for a Subscription. Being the seller means Vink charges, files and pays the VAT itself. **Stripe Tax** works it out: every Checkout has `automatic_tax`, asks for the billing address and takes an optional VAT number (`tax_id_collection`), and saves both on the Customer, so renewals and the next Top-up are taxed the same. A Dutch business pays 21%; a business elsewhere in the EU with a VAT number gets reverse charge. Stripe Tax goes by the number's format and checks it against VIES only afterwards, so the webhook removes a number VIES calls unverified (later invoices then carry VAT) and logs it, because the invoices already sent need a look by hand. Prices are excl. VAT (`tax_behavior: "exclusive"`), as on the Pricing page, and every Product has the SaaS tax code. Stripe Tax collects VAT only where the account has an active registration, so the registrations in the Dashboard decide what is charged; `scripts/stripe-setup.mts` fails without an active Dutch one, and warns when EU buyers without a VAT number would pay none (no small seller scheme, no OSS).

**Payment methods** come from one Payment Method Configuration that `scripts/stripe-setup.mts` makes: card, Apple Pay, Google Pay, iDEAL, SEPA Direct Debit and Bancontact on, everything else off. Checkout gets the configuration, never a list of payment method types (API `2026-09-30.endive` no longer takes one), and shows each method only where it fits: Bancontact for Top-ups only, iDEAL for a Plan only with "iDEAL recurring payments" on in the Dashboard for the "Vink" configuration itself, not just the Default one (renewals then run as SEPA Direct Debit), and Google Pay not at all while Stripe Tax has no shipping address to go on. A bank payment can succeed days later. A Top-up counts only once its Checkout Session is `paid` (`checkout.session.completed` or `checkout.session.async_payment_succeeded`). A Subscription paid by SEPA Direct Debit is `active` while its first payment is on its way, so the Plan starts at once; if that payment bounces (`checkout.session.async_payment_failed`, or a voided first invoice), Stripe leaves the Subscription active and its first invoice open, so Vink cancels the one and voids the other, and the Plan goes. A new Plan Checkout expires any other one still open, so two tabs can't make two Subscriptions.

Stripe is the source of truth for the **Subscription**; Vink never decides who pays. Every webhook (`convex/billing.ts`) reads the Customer's Subscriptions back from Stripe and hands the live Plan to `billingState:applySubscription`, which is idempotent and ignores a read older than the last one it applied, so retried and out-of-order webhooks are harmless. Subscriptions without a Plan's Price (made in the Dashboard) are left alone. Vink makes the Stripe Customer at an Organisation's first Checkout and finds the Organisation through it.

Vink stays the source of truth for the **Pages**. A Plan's Pages are per month also when it's billed annually, so a Pages period is not Stripe's billing period: it is monthly, on the day of the Subscription's billing anchor, and the `pages periods` cron renews it as before. A change of Plan keeps the period and the Pages used, with the new allowance. The Customer Portal moves to a smaller Plan or to monthly billing only at the end of the period, and a cancelled Plan runs to the end of its period; after that the Organisation is back on whatever Free Pages it has left, and its Top-ups lapse. A failed renewal keeps the Plan while Stripe retries (`past_due`); when Stripe gives up (`unpaid` or cancelled), the Plan goes.

The code knows Prices by **lookup key** (`vink_<plan>_<monthly|annual>`, `vink_topup_100`), never by ID, so one codebase serves a sandbox and live mode. `scripts/stripe-setup.mts` makes the catalog, the payment methods, the Customer Portal configuration and the webhook endpoint in either; a new price in `lib/plans.ts` becomes a new Price that takes over the lookup key, and existing Subscriptions keep the old one.

## Consequences

- Each Convex deployment needs `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` (a restricted key), `STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET`, `STRIPE_PAYMENT_METHOD_CONFIGURATION` and `STRIPE_PORTAL_CONFIGURATION` for its own Stripe environment: the dev deployment a sandbox, prod live mode.
- Stripe Tax needs the head office address and the right registrations in the Dashboard. We register the Netherlands under the **small seller** scheme (2026-10-06): Vink sells less than €10,000 a year to EU buyers without a VAT number, so they pay Dutch VAT and no OSS is needed. Above that threshold the registration becomes OSS.
- Vink files the VAT returns; Stripe Tax's reports are the input.
- Stripe Tax costs 0.5% per transaction where Vink is registered, on top of the payment fees and Billing's 0.7%; a Top-up's invoice (`invoice_creation`) costs 0.4%, at most US$2.
- iDEAL requires the KvK number on the website.
- The runtime key needs write on Checkout Sessions, Customers (to remove an unverified VAT number), Subscriptions and Invoices (to cancel one whose first payment bounced and void its invoice) and Customer portal, and read on Products and Prices. The setup script's key also needs write on Products, Prices, Payment Method Configurations and Webhook Endpoints, and read on Tax settings and registrations.
